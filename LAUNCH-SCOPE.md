Version: 0.2
Date: 7 October 2026
Status: First-release baseline adopted under D-16. Actual publishing inputs, specific assessment gates and human reviews remain outstanding.

## Product promise and scope boundary

Help a learner understand crypto, judge when it is appropriate, practise everyday decisions in fictional situations, and keep a clear learning journey. Help a reader understand news, coins and wider crypto stories without first taking a course. A wider African audience with a West African focus, English with occasional varied Nigerian Pidgin, WordPress, free core reading, optional accounts, 30-day opt-in guest memory and account article bookmarks are approved direction.

The owner adopted this first-release baseline from v0.1 under D-16. [Decision Log](DECISION-LOG.md) records that explicit response and its limits. Counts/settings/assets are settled product scope; individual publishable copy, assessment keys/gates and country/provider claims still need review. [Regional research](REGIONAL-SCOPE-RESEARCH.md) distinguishes examples from current operational applicability. [Assessment Rules](ASSESSMENT-RULES.md) and [Editorial Operating Plan](EDITORIAL-OPERATING-PLAN.md) specify the associated work, not completed production.

This is documentation-only. No code, component installation/configuration, purchase, deployment or visual design is commissioned. Later delivery owns styling and working UI/accessibility checks. Approving scope does not cause the planning assistant to implement it.

2. **A-EX-02:** Who controls a wallet, and what can be recovered? — conditional custody models, no universal phrase/password rule.

## Assets, regions and numerical examples

Propose **BTC/Bitcoin, ETH/Ethereum and USDC** as the initial named asset examples and reviewed asset hubs. Their different roles support the foundation's asset/network/token distinctions and existing inspected sources. This is a maintainability/teaching judgement, not a ranking, holding recommendation or promise that they are available through a local route. USDC is one issuer-specific stablecoin example; teach broader model differences without generalising its terms to every token.

Ethereum and Base can illustrate distinct sending networks where the current lesson sources support the distinction. The earlier Circle deployment evidence includes an excerpt-only limit; exact current contract/support claims need fuller verification before publication. Bitcoin, native ETH and different token representations must have separate identities. Provider services remain fictional in practice; no commercial service is endorsed or selected.

Nigeria and Ghana are proposed settings/research anchors. Fictional NGN/GHS figures can make cost comparisons familiar, with a visible invented-input label; they are not live rates, payout quotes or proof of an available/legal route. The foundation remains broadly readable. Actual country-specific legal, wage/invoice, provider, bank/mobile-money or payout instructions require current applicable source/subject review. The February Ghana FAQ creates an explicit review issue for crypto wage/invoice examples; do not treat generic freelance examples as permitted Ghana instructions.

Review comprehension with non-Nigerian West African readers and, where access permits, another West African context and wider-African comparison. Francophone discovery can inform future French scope without pretending English-first publication serves every language equally. Initial research settings are not a site-access restriction or verified supported-country list.

## Motivation and later scope

Launch motivation comes from useful decisions, varied fictional situations, explanatory feedback, visible progress and optional practice. Celebrate a well-reasoned pause/decline as appropriate. The title may change after brand review; business naming is not resolved by the repository name.

Keep advanced DeFi, trading/derivatives, technical building, deep consensus/cryptography, specialist application courses, real-time analytical tools, automated tutors, community moderation, leaderboards/reward economies, wallet/exchange integrations, paid memberships and French publication as separate later decisions. These topics can appear in reviewed standalone articles or glossary context without creating a hidden course/tool promise. The 43-topic living map keeps broader coverage visible for research and future tracks.
