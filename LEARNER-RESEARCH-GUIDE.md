# Wetin Be Crypto — Learner Research Guide

Version: 0.3
Date: 7 October 2026
Status: Proposed session protocol and reusable note templates. No participants have been recruited, interviewed, or tested through this guide.
Work phases: P-01/P-02 content review and P-03/P-05 written functional-flow review. Visual design is excluded under approved D-15.

## Purpose and authority

Investigate actual learner situations, review representative teaching, and check whether people understand the proposed reading, practice, progress, and saving experience. This guide prepares sessions; it does not supply learner findings or authorise outreach.

Read [Research and Planning Process](RESEARCH-AND-DESIGN-PLAN.md), [Decision Log](DECISION-LOG.md), [Product Brief](PRODUCT-BRIEF.md), [Curriculum and Journeys](CURRICULUM-AND-JOURNEYS.md), and [Feature Specification](FEATURE-SPECIFICATION.md) alongside this guide. Record recommendations in the [Evidence Register](EVIDENCE-REGISTER.md) after actual reviews occur.

Preserve approved decisions D-01 to D-15. In particular:

- The audience is wider Africa with a West African focus; country coverage remains proposed.
- Practical understanding and safe everyday use lead the foundation. Reading is open; only selected practice has declared prerequisite checks.
- English carries essential meaning. Occasional, varied Nigerian Pidgin is contextual; French readiness does not mean a French application exists.
- An optional account saves learning across devices. Guests can opt into current-device memory for 30 days after their last learning activity; ordinary article reading does not extend that period.
- Reading completion, passed checks, demonstrated practice, and recommended review are separate records. Article reads/bookmarks award no learning credit.
- Joining an existing account offers a choice about importing eligible guest activity. Simple account-only article bookmarks are included.

Research may identify a reason to propose a policy change. It does not silently replace an approved policy.

## Proposed participants and access

Use a small purposive first round of **six to eight participants**, not a representative sample. These proposed groups describe situations and can overlap:

| Situation | Suggested places in the round | Useful context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Curious beginners | 2–3 | Little or no crypto use; can describe what they want to understand. |
| Practical learners | 2 | Have encountered a payment, receipt, transfer, wallet, conversion, or stablecoin question. |
| Existing users | 1–2 | Have used crypto and can review gaps or an alternative-entry challenge. |
| Primarily article readers | 1 | Interested in understanding coverage; may choose to remain readers. |

**Geographic proposal:** start with Nigeria and Ghana; include another West African context, such as Senegal or Côte d'Ivoire, and a wider-African comparison where participant access permits. Do not turn this into a supported-country list. Record actual current country/residency context; nationality alone does not establish someone's payment experience.

Aim for at least two non-Nigerian West African readers when checking Pidgin comprehension, a fluent Nigerian reader for naturalness, and variation in gender, phone use, shared-device use, and connection/data conditions. These are recruitment aims, not fulfilled quotas or assumptions that a country's learners share one experience. Record missed coverage explicitly rather than substitute a desk-research claim.

Include a Francophone West African discovery interview if language support is available. Record the person's preferred language and English reading comfort. A French interview or interpreter-supported session is different from an independent English-content task; report them separately. Do not infer that everyone in a Francophone country has the same language needs or label a language mismatch as a design-task failure.

### Access and recruitment assumptions

- The owner supplies or arranges volunteers and determines recruitment channels, scheduling, any fixed incentive, facilitator, and reviewer access. This guide does not contact anyone.
- Propose adults for the first round. Participant age eligibility and any different research protocol need owner agreement before recruitment.
- Participants can join in person or remotely and use an ordinary device where practical. No crypto ownership, money, account creation, wallet connection, or identity documents are needed.
- State the activity neutrally as reviewing crypto learning and reading examples. Do not recruit on the promise of earnings or call it a test of financial ability.
- A fixed incentive, if offered, should not depend on favourable feedback or task success.
- Use participant IDs such as LR-01. Keep recruitment contact details separately with the owner; do not put them in this repository.

### Short screening questions for the owner/facilitator

1. Which country are you currently using digital services from? Is another country's context relevant to your recent experience?
2. Which language would you prefer for a conversation? How comfortable are you reading short English explanations?
3. What, if anything, have you recently tried to understand or do with crypto? Broad descriptions are enough.
4. Which device would you normally use for reading or learning? Do you share it?
5. What connection or data limitations would we need to accommodate for a short review?
6. Would you be comfortable reviewing fictional examples and giving candid feedback? No real funds or account access are involved.

Use [Content Design Samples](CONTENT-DESIGN-SAMPLES.md) once the named examples are available and ready for review. Record the exact version/commit and exposure order in each session.

| --- | --- |
| L-04.2 / O-04.2 | Lesson and outcome: network and receiving-route compatibility. |
| G-NETWORK | Glossary definition used with the lesson. |
| KC-04 | Knowledge check, criteria, explanations, and retry variant. |
| PM-04 | Fictional compatibility mission and outcome rubric. |
| A-EX-01 | Standalone evergreen explainer and learning connection. |
| A-NEWS-01 | Sourced archive news summary: source, event/publication context, claims, and dates. |
| H-STABLECOINS | Topic introduction and routes to articles/learning. |

The content pack is a review artifact, not a working website. Confirm the lesson/check/mission's source review and declared criteria before using them to judge an answer. Where a rubric or link is absent, record it as unavailable and use exploratory comprehension questions instead of awarding a pass.

Prepare separate learner-facing assessment pages and facilitator answer/rubric pages. Show feedback after the participant's first submitted response; do not expose the answer key during independent practice. Deliberately invalid placeholders and fictional figures must be clearly identified. Do not use a valid recovery phrase, real recipient, participant account, or live transaction.

For saving/progress tasks, prepare labelled **proposed-state cards** for: current-visit guest; device-memory offer; remembered guest; expired guest; new-account save; existing-account import choice; journey records; and save pending/failed. These may be written descriptions now and annotated screens later. A described card is not an implemented state. Use the approved FS behaviour; label unresolved visit/storage details rather than inventing them.

Before a session, the facilitator should:

- Select one content route and the continuity tasks; avoid making every person review everything.
- Check accessible delivery: readable document, sufficient text size, keyboard access to the artifact, and alternatives to optional media.
- Remove notification/account information from facilitator screens; ask participants not to display personal accounts.
- Prepare a note-taking ID, consent script, stopping point, and rubric. Pilot the guide with an internal reviewer; label that pilot as expert review, not learner research.
- Confirm the owner-approved privacy notice, contact for withdrawing contributions, note/recording storage, and retention period before collecting information.

## Consent and opening script

Use plain language and pause for the participant's response:

> We are reviewing examples for a proposed crypto learning website. We are testing the material and proposed experience, not your ability. There is no working application in this review. Please tell us what you understand and what feels unclear; disagreement is useful. We will use fictional information and will not ask for money, passwords, recovery phrases, or personal account access.
>
> Taking part is voluntary. You may skip a question or stop at any time. We would like to take anonymised notes. An anonymised summary may be included in the project's GitHub planning documents. We will not include your name, contact information, account information, or identifying screenshots. [Explain who holds the notes, their retention period, and the withdrawal contact and cutoff from the agreed privacy notice.] Is it all right to continue and take notes?

Ask separately about any recording, its use, storage, and deletion. A refusal to record does not prevent a notes-only session. Do not record until the participant expressly agrees and the storage/retention arrangements are defined. Only publish an attributed or potentially identifying quotation if it has separate, explicit permission; anonymised paraphrases are preferable for this package.

## Suggested session structure

Plan roughly **45–60 minutes**, adjusting to participant access and fatigue. This is a scheduling proposal, not an optimal lesson-length claim.

1. Opening and consent: 3–5 minutes.
2. Recent-experience interview: 10–15 minutes.
3. Content route: 20–25 minutes. Choose learning tasks T-01 to T-03, or reading tasks T-04/05 with a relevant lesson sample if useful.
4. Progress/saving scenarios: 8–12 minutes; select the highest-value T-06 to T-09 cases for this participant.
5. Voice check and debrief: 3–5 minutes, including observations already made while reading.

Do not force the schedule. If content review runs long, defer another task and record it as not attempted. Distribute tasks across the round and record which received actual coverage. Rotate route/order where practical; earlier explanations can teach later answers, so record prior exposure.

## Interview guide: actual situations first

Start with recent experience before showing the product promise. Use neutral questions; do not name a suspected problem until the participant describes it.

| Theme | Main prompt | Neutral follow-ups |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recent situation | Tell me about the last time you wanted to understand something involving crypto. | What prompted it? What did you do first? Where did you stop? |
| Practical use | If you have tried a payment, receipt, wallet, or conversion, talk me through the steps in broad terms. | What did you expect to happen? What information helped? What remained unclear? |
| Beginner context | If you have not used crypto, what made you curious or cautious? | What would you need to understand before deciding whether it is useful? Choosing not to use it is a valid answer. |
| Sources and trust | Where did you look for an explanation, and how did you decide whether to trust it? | Was there a claim you could not check? How did advice from other people affect your decision? |
| Vocabulary | Which words or screens took the most effort to understand? | What did you think that term meant? How would you explain it to a friend? |
| Learning continuity | Tell me about something you recently learned online and later returned to. | What helped you find your place? What made you stop? Did you want an account, and why? |
| Reading | Think of a recent crypto story you read or heard about. What did you want to know afterwards? | Did you look for more coverage, a definition, a tutorial, or nothing further? |
| Device/access | How do you normally read or learn on your device? | Does data cost, connection quality, storage, battery, shared use, text size, or input method change what you do? |

Avoid leading prompts such as “Would missions make learning fun?”, “You would want to save your progress, right?”, or “Was that Pidgin confusing?” If someone mentions a loss or difficult experience, accept a broad explanation and offer to move on; do not press for amounts or personal details.

## Task-based review guide

Give the scenario, ask the person to work as they normally would, and invite them to talk through their reasoning if comfortable. Do not identify the expected button, answer, or route. Use “What are you looking for?” or “What would you do next?” before assistance. Record the exact assistance and the point where it occurred.

### T-01 — Understand a lesson and use a definition

**Prompt:** “You have seen a network name in instructions for receiving crypto. Use this material to explain what you would need to know before deciding whether the transfer route is suitable.”

**Materials:** L-04.2, G-NETWORK. **Observe:** terms interpreted, glossary use, worked-example reasoning, and confusion about matching names/address appearance.

**Meaningful outcome:** the learner can explain O-04.2 in their own words using the approved sample criteria. After the first explanation, offer a different fictional example and ask what information they need. Recognise an appropriate decision to stop because information is missing.

**Boundary:** this establishes sample comprehension, not real-world transfer competence. A Markdown link tests document access only; written flows cannot prove website navigation discoverability.

### T-02 — Check understanding and use feedback

**Prompt:** “Try this check as you would on your own. Explain the reason for your answer. After the result, tell me what you would do next.”

**Materials:** KC-04 and its separate rubric/feedback. **Observe:** first response, rationale, essential decisions, feedback interpretation, revision choice, and any retry response. If the first response passes, use a hypothetical unsuccessful result to review feedback wording; keep that separate from the actual attempt.

**Meaningful outcome:** correct rationale and essential decisions under the approved rubric; understanding why a response is or is not sufficient. Record first attempt and assisted/retry outcomes separately.

**Boundary:** do not invent a score threshold or claim a live grading engine. The facilitator applies the declared rubric manually and labels the result as a research review.

### T-03 — Complete independent fictional practice

**Prompt:** “Review these fictional sending and receiving instructions. Decide whether to proceed, pause, or reject the route, and explain which information supports your decision.”

**Materials:** PM-04 with the answer/rubric hidden. **Observe:** inspection order, assumptions, information sought, essential compatibility decision, and whether the learner stops appropriately.

**Meaningful outcome:** meets the sample mission's mapped rubric independently. After recording the initial response, show explanatory feedback and a meaningful variant if one is available. A copied worked-example answer is not independent evidence.

**Boundary:** no actual transfer, wallet connection, provider advice, or proof of durable learning. Earlier lesson/check exposure must appear in the session record.

### T-04 — Read a sourced story without joining

**Prompt:** “Someone shared this report with you. Explain what happened, when it happened, and what you would check before relying on the account. Find any further explanation you would want; you can also choose to stop reading.”

**Materials:** A-NEWS-01, A-EX-01, H-STABLECOINS and their available links. **Observe:** fact/attributed-claim distinction, source/date interpretation, archive recognition, and chosen continuation.

**Meaningful outcome:** the reader understands the summary without taking a course and recognises that the archive sample is not a new event. Following a source, more reporting, an explainer, or optional learning are all valid routes. Report actual clicks separately from intentions.

**Boundary:** do not require a reader to convert into a learner. The content pack cannot establish a functioning feed, live freshness, filter behaviour, or search relevance.

### T-05 — Choose a discovery route and save an article

**Prompt:** “You want to understand stablecoins and later return to a useful article. Show where you would look and explain how you expect to find it again.”

**Materials now:** article/hub copy and proposed search/bookmark-state descriptions. **Materials later:** feed, hub, search/empty-result, account-return, and Saved Articles screens. **Observe:** terms used for search, content-type distinctions, useful destinations, and expectations about saving.

**Meaningful outcome:** finds a relevant destination where the artifact provides one; understands reading remains public and saving an article uses an account; does not expect bookmarking to increase learning progress. A reader may choose not to register.

**Boundary:** if controls/results are only described, record comprehension or intended choice, not successful navigation/search/bookmark persistence.

### T-06 — Interpret progress and choose a next step

**Prompt:** “This fictional journey shows two lessons completed, one check passed, one mission not yet demonstrated, and a review recommendation. Tell me what it says about your learning and what you would do next.”

**Materials:** proposed My Journey state. Include a separate experienced-entry case: a challenge passed, related lessons unread.

**Meaningful outcome:** distinguishes reading from checks/practice; does not treat review as deleted achievement or a challenge pass as invented reading history. Any sensible next step is acceptable; record the reason.

**Boundary:** the facilitator presents fictional records. Their interpretation does not prove saved data, current applicability rules, or cross-device resume.

### T-07 — Choose device memory and explain its limits

**Prompt:** “You are using this device as a guest and may return next week. Read the offered choices and choose what suits you. Explain what you expect if you return on this device, use another device, clear browser storage, or return after 31 days without learning.”

**Materials:** current-visit, opt-in, remembered, clearing, and expired proposed states. After the initial answer, ask: “Suppose you read articles during that period but do no learning activity. What would you expect?”

**Meaningful outcome:** recognises opt-in device memory, the 30-day period after learning activity, device-specific limits, account continuity, and loss of guest evidence after expiry/clearing. Declining memory is valid and is not a failed task. Confusion becomes a copy/design finding, not a reason to alter D-11 automatically.

**Boundary:** expiry/storage cannot be observed from these cards. Do not describe current-visit duration or qualifying actions beyond the approved specification as settled; PD-07 remains open.

### T-08 — Decide whether to import shared-device activity

**Prompt:** “You sign into an existing account on a shared browser. Your account completed A and B; the browser holds guest completions B and C plus a weaker check attempt. Decide what should happen and explain the result you expect.”

**Materials:** proposed existing-account choice and reconciliation outcomes. Do not reveal the outcome card until after the prediction. Separately show a new-account Save my progress case if time allows.

**Meaningful outcome:** understands that browser activity might belong to someone else; can decline import; predicts A/B/C counted once if importing, with the existing pass preserved. New-account saving is understood as carrying applicable current guest work into that account.

**Boundary:** this tests ownership/merge explanations. It cannot prove record validation, deduplication, account security, or recovery after interruption.

### T-09 — Understand a failed save or unmet prerequisite

**Prompt:** “The next state says your progress is not yet confirmed as saved. Tell me what you believe has happened and what you would do next.” Use a separate case: “This practice activity says a required check is missing. How would you continue?”

**Materials:** pending/failed-save and unmet-prerequisite proposed states, including expiry where appropriate.

**Meaningful outcome:** does not mistake pending/failed for confirmed saving; identifies a useful retry/account route; understands that the relevant check can be completed as a guest and that public reading remains available.

**Boundary:** no connection failure, retry, or gate is implemented. Manually switching cards is a simulation; record it that way.

## Voice, accessibility, and low-data review

### Pidgin comprehension and naturalness

Observe a participant's first reading before asking about language preference. Ask: “What does this sentence mean here?” and “Does it change what you would do?” Then ask what feels natural, distracting, patronising, overly casual, or difficult to understand. Do not supply a translation before recording the initial interpretation.

Show an otherwise equivalent plain-English version afterwards when useful. Record exposure order and separate preference from comprehension. Alternating first exposure across this small round can reveal issues; it is not a statistical A/B experiment or proof of a Pidgin retention effect.

- Use non-Nigerian readers to check understanding; use a fluent Nigerian reviewer to check naturalness and repetition across a batch. One person's preference does not represent a country.
- Check that removing an aside removes no definition, instruction, rationale, or necessary feedback.
- Review neutral tone for loss, fraud, allegations, serious security instructions, and corrections. Do not manufacture a participant's personal-loss scenario to test humour.
- Record the exact wording/version. A generic response that the voice is “nice” is insufficient evidence of comprehension.

### Accessibility and connection context

Ask what device/input method and reading adjustments the person ordinarily uses. Offer text-only materials and appropriate alternatives. If the person normally uses keyboard navigation, a screen reader, or magnification, accommodate that method rather than ask them to imitate another user.

Where actual artifact interactions exist, record observed difficulty with headings, links, reading order, focus, text size, and instructions. A Markdown viewer's behaviour belongs to that viewer; it does not establish the eventual WordPress theme's accessibility.

Review the text-only lesson/article without optional media. Ask the learner to explain the core meaning and required action. Record any missing information. Slow-connection/data-cost comments provide context for later budgets; document size or a participant's expectation cannot prove page speed, offline support, save reliability, or WCAG conformance.

## Synthesis and review rules

After real sessions, create a finding record that separates:

1. **Observed evidence:** exact task/material, anonymised action or statement, assistance, and actual exposure count.
2. **Interpretation:** the likely issue or opportunity and plausible alternatives.
3. **Recommendation:** a bounded content/design change linked to RQ, FS/AC, journey, and pending decision IDs.
4. **Confidence and limits:** country/language/sample coverage, artifact fidelity, missing tasks, and whether the finding repeated or had counterexamples.
5. **Disposition:** proposed change, further review, or accepted decision by the actual authorised owner/reviewer.

Use descriptive counts only for tasks actually attempted: for example, “2 of 5 reviewers who saw this card expected cross-device guest saving.” This is an example reporting format, not a research result; such a count cannot be projected to the audience. Separate interviews, expert walkthroughs, assisted interpretations, and independent task observations.

Proposed issue priorities:

| Priority | Meaning in this review |
| --- | --- |
| Critical | Essential instruction/rationale is misleading or permits a materially unsafe decision in the fictional scenario. Hold affected sample assessment use until subject review. |
| High | The intended task cannot be completed, or the person misunderstands account ownership, a save confirmation, required prerequisite, or archive/current status. |
| Medium | Recoverable confusion or unnecessary effort; essential meaning/outcome remains reachable. |
| Low | Tone/preference or minor presentation issue without a demonstrated outcome failure. |

Do not infer that every wrong answer is a content defect. Check prior knowledge, language, reading exposure, rubric ambiguity, moderator assistance, and whether the task itself supplied sufficient information. Independent crypto/source review is necessary when factual teaching is in question; learner preference cannot establish technical truth.

No recommendation overrides D-01 to D-15 without owner approval. Record detailed outcome/threshold, country, interaction, or performance proposals against PD-01/02/04/07/08 rather than claiming the session approved them.

## What each review stage can establish

| Stage/material | Can establish with actual observations | Cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Markdown/content pack | Comprehension of that copy, source/date interpretation, manual rubric responses, actual document-link use, initial voice feedback. | Website discoverability, live grading, persistence, authentication, full accessibility, performance. |
| Written proposed-state cards | Interpretation of saving/progress/import explanations and the person's predicted decision. | Working state transitions, expiry, merging, error recovery, ownership enforcement. |
| Sitemap and written functional flows | Interpretation of described page relationships, route/state copy, and predicted choices; actual document-link actions if observed. | Visual discoverability, actual website navigation, responsive implementation, keyboard/screen-reader conformance, network/save behaviour. |
| Future commissioned working application | Implemented journeys and relevant acceptance criteria when actually exercised under stated conditions. | Broader population demand or durable learning without further suitable study. |

After source/content changes, repeat focused comprehension/rationale tasks where the affected outcome warrants it. Revisit continuity/route explanations using [Information Architecture](INFORMATION-ARCHITECTURE.md) and [Functional Flows](FUNCTIONAL-FLOWS.md) in P-05; actual UI navigation validation belongs to later delivery. A delayed retrieval or varied scenario can investigate retention/transfer if access permits; timing and criteria must be agreed rather than invented from this first round.

1. **Participant access and coverage:** who arranges the six-to-eight-person round, which country/language contexts are realistically available, adult eligibility, any incentive, and whether interpreter/accessibility support is available.
