# Wetin Be Crypto — Voice and Editorial Guide

Version: 0.2
Date: 7 October 2026
Status: Draft P-02 guide. Owner-approved voice/publishing direction; example wording, operational workflow, and capacity require review.

## Voice promise

Explain crypto accurately in clear English. Use familiar situations, useful questions, and occasional contemporary Nigerian Pidgin to give the writing warmth. The audience spans Africa with a West African focus; a reader should not need to understand Pidgin to follow a required instruction.

The teaching should sound like someone helping you inspect a situation, think through it, and ask better questions. Avoid promises of profit, status, or certainty to make a lesson exciting. Learning rewards recognise understanding and sound decisions, including pausing or declining.

## Pidgin with context and variety

- Start from what the sentence needs to do: invite attention, reinforce an idea, express empathy, or make an example memorable.
- Write the essential explanation in English. An aside can add flavour; removing it must remove no definition, criterion, instruction, or required rationale.
- There is no phrase quota, fixed rotation, or requirement that every lesson uses Pidgin. Common words such as wahala can recur naturally; inspect whether a batch feels repetitive rather than ban a word or swap synonyms mechanically.
- Vary the placement and purpose as well as wording. Repeating the same opening/closing structure with different phrases still sounds scripted.
- Review with a fluent Nigerian speaker and non-Nigerian readers. Record what they actually saw and understood; the assistant's draft is not that review.

Illustrative lines, not mandatory templates:

| Context | Draft example | Essential meaning remains elsewhere |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invite inspection of a worked example. | “Before you press Send, make we check the route.” | English explains the exact checks. |
| A reader has understood a distinction. | “You don catch the difference.” | Feedback states the correct reasoning. |
| Invite the learner's reasoning. | “If na you, wetin you go verify first?” | The task gives an English question and criteria. |
| Reveal a consequence of terms. | “Small print fit change the whole story.” | The article explains the conditions. |
| Support a retry. | “Try am again; the clue dey for the receiving instructions.” | English explains the mistake and useful next action. |

These examples need human naturalness review. Do not paste them across the inventory as a phrase bank.

## Tone by situation

| Situation | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| Concept explanation / fictional everyday example | Conversational, precise, with useful comparisons and occasional light humour. |
| Feedback | Explain the decision and reason; offer a next step. Celebrate only the demonstrated outcome. |
| Prerequisite, expiry, or save problem | State what happened, what remains available, and the next action. Avoid blame, guilt, or false saving reassurance. |
| Loss, fraud, allegations, serious security instructions, corrections | Direct, restrained English. Avoid jokes or catchphrases that minimise the consequence. |
| News | Facts and attributed claims first; optional interpretation clearly framed. Familiarity must not weaken accuracy. |

Avoid compulsory streak language or pressure to transact. Prefer “Continue when you're ready” to implying that a break erased the learner's achievements. Account benefits explain saving rather than interrupt public reading repeatedly.

## Editorial formats

| Format | Reader promise | Required content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| News summary | Understand a specific development and its source/context. | Event/source/publication dates as relevant; attribution; supporting links; known limits; material corrections. |
| Explainer | Understand a durable concept independently of a course. | Clear question, original explanation, practical example, sources, verification responsibility, useful further reading. |
| Story | Understand an experience or case and its decisions. | Source/consent context where needed; distinguish anecdote from typical results; country/time scope; avoid implied endorsement. |
| Asset/topic hub | Orient and connect reviewed coverage. | Canonical identity, introduction, uses/limitations, sources/review status, and published relationships. |
| Lesson / assessment | Explain and assess a stated outcome. | Outcome/version, sources, example, criteria, independent variation, explanatory feedback, and change dependencies. |

Stories and original explainers are original writing. News summaries are also editorially written; source links are not permission to copy a full article. A press release establishes what its issuer announced or claims, not independent confirmation of every promised benefit.

## Source and date rules

Record exact source, publisher, actual inspection date, source date/version/country when available, claim supported, and limitations. Mark excerpt-only access. Attribute claims about reserve backing, product support, measured use, or effectiveness to the evidence that establishes them. Do not copy promotional certainty or unsupported rankings/yields into educational voice.

Keep event date, source publication date, our actual publication date, verification date, and material-update date distinct. A punctuation edit is not a new event or a fresh substantive verification. Archive summaries must remain recognisable as archives when shown in a feed. Document-preparation dates must not masquerade as site publication.

A reference to a country can be setting or material applicability. State which; avoid implying a regional route works because a fictional character lives there. Confirm country/provider facts before publishing practical instructions.

## Review workflow proposal

1. Writer prepares original copy, exact source/claim records, relationships, and proposed status/date labels.
2. Relevant source/crypto reviewer checks mechanics, asset/representation, custody/network/provider/country scope, and assessment correctness.
3. English/voice review checks clarity, context, repetition, and comprehension. Fluent Nigerian and non-Nigerian review applies to actual sampled wording; staffing remains open.
4. Publishing editor approves a named revision, sources, labels, and relationships. Material claim changes require renewed relevant review.
5. Publication records actual dates and responsibility. Substantive updates retain the approved public revision until the new one is approved under the selected workflow.
6. Corrections record what changed and when if material, and identify dependent lessons, question versions, hubs, and later translations. Preserve historical attempts while correcting current applicability.

These are responsibilities, not selected WordPress roles or a verified approval system. Staffing may combine responsibilities where appropriate; independence expectations and technical enforcement require PD-03/06 review. Emergency corrections need the separately specified editor-authorised audit/review route.

## Batch review checklist

- Does the explanation establish the outcome and preserve exact asset/network/custody distinctions?
- Are fact, attribution, interpretation, fictional inputs, and country applicability understandable?
- Does each article stand alone while offering useful optional links?
- Are terms, dates, sources, revision responsibility, and correction dependencies present?
- Are the Pidgin asides natural, varied in function/placement, and understandable? Are stock openings or closings repeated?
- Does removing an aside preserve every required meaning?
- Are assessment criteria and feedback fair, versioned, and limited to mapped outcomes?
- Are actual source/voice/learner reviews distinguished from pending reviews?

The checklist is for content review. It does not prove implemented publication enforcement, saving, discovery, accessibility, or learner effectiveness.
